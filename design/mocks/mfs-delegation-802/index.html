Helping people pick where to stake

Issue #802 ↗ Design decisions ↓ Open questions ↓ Who can use this ↓

In Livepeer 2.0, a node’s new LPT rewards follow the smaller of two shares: how much work it does (fees), and how much stake sits behind it. Cash for that work stays whole. Stake unlocks more LPT only while the node is doing more work than it has stake for.

These mocks are for the moment of choice. If you are picking a node, you should feel where new stake still earns. If you are already bonded, you should know whether yours still has room, and what moving would cost. Plain language carries the story; the math waits in tooltips for anyone who wants it.

Color maps quantity (Livepeer brand palette). Fees · blue and Stake · green stay fixed; the state chip takes the color of whichever side is smaller. Green means stake, not “good.” Emissions are still settling, so the numbers here are shares of the network: how far a ceiling can still move.

Loading state mirrors row height:
  • Labels: “Room to earn,” “Full,” and “Balanced” name what happens if you stake here. A “caps rewards” tag marks the limiting side so the ceiling is visible at a glance.
  • Filter: “Earning now” keeps the list focused on nodes with real activity. “All nodes” keeps every address reachable when you need a specific one. It only changes which nodes appear. The columns stay the same.
  • Familiar numbers: Forecasted yield, Delegated stake, and Trailing 90D fees stay after the decision columns. Stake amounts use the green LPT mark. Fee amounts use the USDC mark. Column order is the hierarchy. Yield is a guess from current shares, so it informs the choice but never sets the order. On narrower laptops the quietest columns drop first (Node keeps, then 90D fees, then Delegated stake). Yield stays.

  • Where it sits: first item in the Orchestrating tab’s two-column grid, spanning both columns of the left content area. Existing stats stay below. The Delegate box stays in the right rail. Not a replacement for that box, and not a third column.
  • Two jobs: the card answers whether this node still has room (network fee share vs stake share). The right-rail box is the checkout for your amount: forecasted yield, inflationary rewards, and fee share for the LPT you type. Same word “fees,” different math.
  • Delegate on the card: optional. On desktop it moves focus to the amount box already on screen. Under 1200px it opens the existing bottom drawer. No stepped flow that hides the box.
  • Same slot, other states: Full and Balanced match the framed card width (shared with the Delegating page frame). Each one only swaps that top slot; the rail and stats stay put.
  • Wide-slot compare: twin fee/stake tiles carry the numbers (same idea as mobile). One short outcome line says what that means for new stake. No prose that restates the same %s again.
  • Collapsed breakdown: the open question is whether stake still earns. How the cut divides between node and delegators waits until someone is ready to commit.
  • Two pots: new LPT and USDC fees are separate rewards, so each lists its own split. A single “you get 92%” figure would blur pots that do not share a cut.

Design decisions

  • Network shares. How much new LPT the network mints is still being finalized, so these screens show how far a node’s reward ceiling can still move (for example from 2.3% toward 11.8%). That keeps the compare about opportunity while emissions settle.
  • Seven settled rounds. The list defaults to the last week of settled work. That window is harder to game, and it lines up with when rewards are paid. Per-round receipts are 803 on History · Rewards.
  • Room to earn / Full / Balanced. Those names speak to the stake choice on the compare list. Under 2.0 the old “Active” label loses its meaning with the capped set. On /delegating, the same stake-capped state reads “You’re earning” instead: the user already bonded, so the copy is about their stake, not leftover room for new stake.
  • Day one is a checklist. The launch card names where work comes from (gateways), then fees, then stake, with brand green and the Livepeer mark as the backdrop.
  • About 21 days to move. That is the working assumption here. If the protocol keeps instant transfers, the note comes out before ship. The wording is ready to follow the rule.
  • Fee and stake only. Honesty scores and validator set design ( 804) stay out. This review stays with where stake still earns.
  • Same controls as the rest of Explorer. Delegate and the amount action are the product button. Focus is a green ring. The phone list and the day-one steps each sit in one panel.
  • History is 803. This ticket owns live fee-vs-stake status. Past payouts and “why that round paid that amount” live on account History · Rewards ( 803). Orchestrating only carries a week-later handoff line.
  • One vocabulary. List, mobile, and detail all mark the limiting side with “caps rewards,” and describe leftover room as “new stake still earns, up to +X%.” Detail uses the same twin fee / stake tiles as mobile, sized for the wide Orchestrating slot. Shared status icons with 803 (trend-up / filled circle / scale).

Open questions

  • Moving stake (~21 days). Design assumes a delayed move so stake cannot flee a penalized node. If moving stake stays instant, delete the lockup note rather than leave a lie. Flag for protocol.
  • APY / dollar mint. Emissions are still a draft. Recommended default: show share math and ceiling movement (for example 2.3% toward 11.8%). Do not ship APY, “5× daily rewards,” or a dollar mint until emissions are fixed.
  • Single-round sort. Resist sorting the list on one unsettled round even if the subgraph makes it easy. Spikes are the attack the seven-round window is meant to blunt.
  • Old “Active” label. Today it means “in the capped orchestrator set.” After 2.0 that set goes away. Shipping fee/stake states under the old status teaches the wrong model.
  • Collapsed cut breakdown. Whether stake still earns is the open question on Orchestrating. How the cut divides waits until someone is ready to commit. Confirm that collapsed-by-default is the right default for stakeholders.

Who can use this

These screens should work for people who cannot rely on color alone, who navigate with a keyboard, who use a screen reader, or who prefer less motion. Same rules apply when this ships in the product.

  • Color is not the only cue. “Room to earn,” “Full,” and “Balanced” each have a word label, a distinct icon, and a 1.5px border. The tile that caps rewards uses a full 1.5px brand border plus wash. Filters and the Delegate / Undelegate choice keep that 1.5px border when selected. Fee and stake shares are spoken as numbers. LPT amounts wear the green mark. Fee amounts wear the USDC mark.
  • Keyboard. Filters and Delegate / Undelegate can be moved with the arrow keys. Focus is a green ring on the control, including the amount field and the help buttons.
  • Screen readers. Choosing a filter or action is announced as a real choice group. Opening the amount drawer puts you on the amount field. Escape closes an open help tip first. If the drawer is open and no tip is, Escape closes it and returns you to the control that opened it. The LPT and USDC marks are named.
  • Help without a mouse. The “?” tips open when you tab to them, and you can move the pointer onto the tip. Escape closes that tip until you point at it or tab to it again. Targets are large enough for touch.
  • Motion. If the system asks for reduced motion, the page does not smooth-scroll, and the buttons do not shift when pressed.
